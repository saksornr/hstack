# Consulting methods: choose by decision

## Diagnose before prescribing

| Decision question | Useful structure | Required evidence | Common misuse |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Why did performance change? | Driver tree and reconciled variance bridge | Comparable periods and consistent definitions | Treating an accounting bridge as proof of causality |
| Where should growth come from? | Customer / offer / channel economics | Segment demand, conversion, contribution, capacity | Calling every growth idea equally attractive |
| Which initiatives deserve resources? | Options table; impact versus effort when sufficient | Defined scoring criteria, cost, dependencies, uncertainty | False precision from arbitrary weighted scores |
| How should execution change? | Process map and operating model | Bottlenecks, handoffs, decision rights, service levels | Redrawing an organization chart without changing work |
| How should we sequence change? | Phased roadmap with stage gates | Dependencies, ownership, adoption, capacity | Dates and arrows without exit criteria |
| Is an opportunity attractive? | Customer, competitor, capability, economics | Explicit customer need and evidence of advantage | Generic SWOT lists without a decision |

Use SWOT, a 2×2, or a three-pillar structure only if it clarifies the choice. Explain axes, categories, scoring, and thresholds. Use qualitative bands where numerical precision is unsupported.

## Issue trees and hypothesis tests

Aim for mutually exclusive branches that collectively cover the defined question. State scope; do not claim universal completeness. Revenue can be active customers × orders per active customer × net revenue per order for a consistent period. Another business may require subscription seats × net revenue per seat or volume × net realized price.

Separate a **driver tree** (what mathematically determines the outcome), a **hypothesis tree** (possible explanations), and an **initiative portfolio** (what to do). Acquisition, retention, and monetization are useful workstreams but can overlap financially.

For a decisive hypothesis, define:
- observation and competing explanations;
- evidence needed, granularity, and source;
- test and the result that would reject the hypothesis;
- decision consequence and next action.

“Customers fell” does not establish whether churn rose, acquisition slowed, reactivation weakened, or tracking changed. Inspect cohorts and consistent customer definitions. Check seasonality, mix, stockouts, price changes, and data quality where relevant.

## Value cases and economics

Build the smallest model that can change the decision. State baseline, counterfactual, incremental volume, unit economics, investment, recurring operating cost, timing, and implementation constraints.

Incremental contribution = incremental net revenue − incremental variable costs.
Net operating benefit = incremental contribution + avoidable cost savings − added operating costs.

Identify transition costs and capital spending separately. Do not equate accounting profit with cash flow. Use NPV only with explicit cash-flow timing and discount-rate assumptions appropriate to the task. Label scenarios as scenarios, not probabilities or forecasts unless supported.

Show downside/base/upside cases for uncertain drivers. Avoid counting the same retained customer in both a retention benefit and an acquisition benefit. Assign interactions once or model the combined effect directly. Capacity released is not cash savings unless spending is actually avoided.

## Options and prioritization

Compare a small number of feasible options using the same criteria. Identify the binding constraint before scoring. A high-value option that cannot meet a hard constraint is not rescued by a weighted average.

If scoring helps, disclose weights and rating definitions. Check whether reasonable changes reverse the ranking. Identify quick experiments that can resolve uncertainty before a large commitment. Distinguish “we recommend testing” from “we recommend scaling.”

## Execution and governance

Specify each initiative's outcome, sponsor, delivery owner, affected teams, milestones, dependencies, resources, adoption plan, and measures. Use role names when actual owners are unknown; label them proposed.

A gate should define evidence, accountable decision-maker, and possible outcomes: proceed, adjust, pause, or stop. Include a review cadence proportional to the pace of change. Report leading indicators alongside lagging outcomes and guardrails.

Example: a reactivation pilot tracks eligible audience, incremental repeat purchase versus a suitable comparison, contribution after incentives, and opt-outs. Scale only if the agreed contribution and customer-experience gates are met. Numerical thresholds must come from evidence or be labeled proposed.
